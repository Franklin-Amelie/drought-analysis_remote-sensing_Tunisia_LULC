# A Remote Sensing Analysis of the Influence of Vegetation and Land Use Types on the Water Cycle in Semiarid Tunisia

### Abstract
Semi-arid regions of North Africa face intensifying drought under ongoing climate change, yet the
influence of land use and land cover (LULC) type on vegetation drought response remains poorly
quantified at the plot level. This study assessed vegetation dynamics and drought sensitivity
across arable land, olive agroforestry, and three forest subclasses — natural forest, Pinus
halepensis, and Eucalyptus sideroxylon — in the Siliana Governorate of northern Tunisia using
nine years of Sentinel-2-derived Soil-Adjusted Vegetation Index (SAVI) and Normalized
Difference Moisture Index (NDMI) from April 2017 to April 2026. Vegetation index anomalies were
correlated with the Standardized Precipitation-Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) across sixteen
timescales using Spearman rank correlation, cross-correlation analysis, Kruskal-Wallis tests, and
linear mixed models. A key methodological finding was that deseasonalisation of vegetation index
time series is essential for meaningful drought correlation analysis in Mediterranean
environments: without seasonal adjustment, the dominant annual phenological cycle suppresses
the interannual drought signal, producing artifactually weak or negative correlations widely
reported in comparable studies.
Following deseasonalisation, land cover classes differed significantly in their optimal SPEI
timescale: agroforestry peaked at SPEI10 to SPEI11, consistent with the progressive soil water
depletion strategy of Olea europaea; natural forest and P. halepensis at SPEI12 to SPEI24,
reflecting multi-year water storage and isohydric stomatal regulation; and E. sideroxylon at
SPEI36 for NDMI, suggesting access to groundwater reserves rather than reliance on soil
moisture. Natural forest exhibited the highest structural resistance and the strongest post-
deseasonalisation correlations, identifying it as the most effective hydrological buffer among the
studied LULC types. Arable land yielded the weakest drought signal due to a fundamental
mismatch between the annual crop cycle and the temporal integration window of cumulative
drought indices. A statistically significant warming trend of 0.04°C per year over 47 years,
combined with a persistently negative water balance during the study period, stresses the
relevance of these findings for land management and afforestation policy in semi-arid North
Africa.
